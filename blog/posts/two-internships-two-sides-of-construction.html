Two internships, two very different sides of construction

Tags: internship, construction, autocad, site-work

Before I finished my degree, I did two internships that could not have been more different — and together they taught me more about how the industry actually works than any classroom ever could.

The design office: Jurutera JRK

My first internship was as an Assistant Engineer at a consultancy. This was the world of drawings, calculations, and meetings — the clean side of construction.

What I actually did:

  • prepared technical drawings — road signage layouts, building plans — in

AutoCAD

  • assisted with basic drainage design and engineering calculations
  • went on site visits to collect data and verify that designs matched

reality

  • coordinated with senior engineers to review and finalize drawings
  • compiled approved drawings into formal documentation and submission

reports

The biggest lesson here wasn't technical. It was that design is a collaborative, iterative process. A drawing goes through review after review. Learning to take feedback on your work — and to communicate clearly with engineers above and below you — is half the job.

The site: Haily Construction

My second internship put me on the other side of the fence entirely, as a Site Supervisor. Same industry, completely different world: boots, safety vests, and the sound of machinery.

What I actually did:

  • supervised daily site operations, making sure work followed the schedule

and specifications

  • monitored worker performance and coordinated site activities to keep the

workflow moving

  • conducted concrete cube testing to verify strength and quality
  • supported project scheduling and tracked task progress
  • managed machinery permit applications and coordinated material purchasing

and delivery

Site work taught me that a good design is only as good as its execution. The people pouring concrete, tying rebar, and operating machinery are the ones who make it real — and respecting their craft makes you a better engineer.

What the two worlds share

Design office or construction site, the same fundamentals matter:

  1. Communication. Whether it's a drawing comment or a morning briefing,

clarity prevents costly mistakes.

  1. Schedules. Time is money — on a drawing board and on a crane.
  2. Quality. It's a cube test on site, a revision check in the office,

but the goal is identical: build it right.

If you're a student deciding between consultancy and contracting for your internship, my advice: do both if you can. The design office teaches you how things should be built. The site teaches you how they actually get built. You need both perspectives to be a complete engineer.
